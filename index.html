01 / THE SPIDER

One network. Multiple lenses.

Signals are observations, not trades. The spider's job is to turn a noisy launch stream into measurable evidence before any entry is considered.

01 — RADARMarket discovery

Observe new listings and indexed Solana activity. Record where and when the signal appeared.

02 — WALLETFlow intelligence

Index observed wallet buys and clusters. Rankings are research inputs, not proof of wallet profits.

03 — GUARDRoute & risk

Evaluate liquidity, quotes, estimated transaction costs and signal freshness. Reject weak setups.

04 — PAPERExecution models

Compare simulated entries and exits without signing a transaction or using real money.

05 — BRAINFeedback loop

Record outcomes, classify failure modes, and test challenger ideas against later evidence.

System map illustrates the research pipeline. It is not a replay of actual blockchain transactions or a claim that every component is currently online.

04 / THE METHOD

Measure first. Evolve second.

Every useful advancement should come from repeatable research, not a dramatic screenshot or a favorable outlier.

01 / OBSERVE

Gather raw market evidence.

Market feeds and indexed wallets supply candidates. A signal is only an invitation to test, not a buy command.

02 / EVALUATE

Model executable costs.

Route checks, estimated slippage, quote availability and position sizing determine whether a proposed paper entry is worth evaluating.

03 / COMPARE

Use controlled paper trials.

Challenger policies are judged on forward evidence, with real pauses, zero-trade periods and failure modes preserved in the underlying research record.

04 / DOCUMENT

Feed the Obsidian brain.

Python research roles categorize outcomes; Qwen and experimental model tools can critique evidence under bounded permissions.
